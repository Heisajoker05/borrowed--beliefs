About This Book

I did not start thinking because I wanted to be a philosopher. I started thinking because I noticed something.

In a community, two different people can look at the same thing and believe two completely different things.

One says school is the way. Another says school is a scam. Both of them have reasons that trigger their beliefs.

This book is not here to tell you what to believe. It is about asking questions.

Why do we believe what we believe? Where did our beliefs come from? And what happens when we question them?

Chapters

Chapter 1 — Hard Work Always Pays

What exactly do we mean when we say that hard work always pays? Is hard work alone enough to guarantee success?

Chapter 2 — Always Be Loyal to Your Friends

What does loyalty really mean? Should loyalty remain when a friend is wrong?

Chapter 3 — Borrowed Beliefs

How many of our beliefs are actually ours? And how many were simply inherited from family, society, religion, friends or culture?

The Question

Before accepting a belief simply because everyone around us accepts it, perhaps we should stop and ask:

"Why do I believe this?"

That question is where the journey begins.

The Idea

We all believe things. Some beliefs come from our parents, our community, our religion, our experiences, our teachers, or the people around us. But how often do we stop and ask: Why do I believe this?

Borrowed Beliefs: Questions Socrates Asked Me explores the beliefs we inherit without always examining them. Inspired by the Socratic method, this project is not about telling you what to believe.

It is about asking better questions, examining our reasons, challenging assumptions, and discovering whether the beliefs we carry are truly ours.

The goal is simple: don't just inherit your beliefs. Understand them.
